How we work

Five steps take your project from first idea to planning approval and building control. We manage the council for you at every stage.

  1. 1Initial consultation

    We meet at your home, talk through your ideas and check what is possible, including permitted development.

  2. 2Measured survey

    We carry out a full measured survey of the property.

  3. 3Design and drawings

    Existing and proposed plans drawn in CAD, with draft options to help you settle the design.

  4. 4Planning submission

    We submit the application and manage it with the council, keeping you informed throughout.

  5. 5Building control

    Once approved, we prepare detailed building regulation drawings so your builder can work with confidence.

Common questions

Planning permission

Do I always need planning permission for an extension?

Not always. Many extensions can be built under permitted development rights. As a guide, a single storey rear extension can go out 3 metres on a terraced or semi-detached house and 4 metres on a detached house, or up to 6 and 8 metres through the council's prior approval process. These rights are restricted in conservation areas, on listed buildings and where earlier extensions have used them up. We check your position at the start.

How long does planning permission take?

Councils aim to decide householder applications within 8 weeks of validating them, and major applications within 13 weeks. Some councils take longer in busy periods. We follow up with the planning officer on your behalf and keep you updated.

What happens if planning permission is refused?

You can appeal to the Planning Inspectorate (for householder applications, within 12 weeks of the decision), revise the design to deal with the reasons for refusal and apply again, or ask the council for pre-application advice. We will recommend the best route based on the reasons given.

What is a pre-application enquiry?

A request to the council for its view on a proposal before a full application is made. It is usually paid. It can be worth it for larger or sensitive schemes, and we can tell you whether it is worthwhile for yours and prepare it for you.

Extensions and lofts

Can I extend a semi-detached house?

Yes. Single storey rear extensions are often possible under permitted development. Side and two storey extensions usually need planning permission. We will go through the options for your property when we meet.

Do I need planning permission for a loft conversion?

Many loft conversions are permitted development, provided the extra roof space is no more than 40 cubic metres on a terraced house or 50 cubic metres on a detached or semi-detached house. Dormers facing the road need permission, and so do most roof changes in conservation areas.

Working with us

How much does it cost?

Fees depend on the type and size of the project. We give you a fixed quote once we understand what you want to do, covering everything from the survey to the planning decision. Council application fees are paid separately. You can get a guide on our fees page.

Do you prepare building control drawings?

Yes. After planning approval we prepare the detailed drawings needed for building regulations, including construction details, specifications and drainage, and coordinate structural calculations where needed. These are quoted separately.

Can you recommend a builder?

Yes. We have worked with reliable builders across North London and Hertfordshire for many years and are happy to suggest some when you reach the build stage.

Which areas do you cover?

Barnet, Enfield, Hertsmere, Harrow, Welwyn Hatfield, East Herts and the surrounding areas. We regularly work in Cockfosters, New Barnet, East Barnet, Hadley Wood, Southgate, Winchmore Hill, Finchley, Mill Hill, Potters Bar, Borehamwood and Hatfield. If you are unsure, just call.
