Getting started

Where do I start?

With a phone call or an email. Tell us a little about the property and what you have in mind, and we will arrange to visit. Call 07843 561129 or send an enquiry.

Do you come to the property?

Yes. The first meeting is at your home, so we can see the house, the garden and the neighbours' properties, listen to what you want and tell you honestly what is likely to be possible.

What do I need to have ready?

Nothing formal. It helps to know roughly what you want, what matters most to you and what you would like to spend on the build. Photos of homes or rooms you like are useful too. If you have old plans, deeds or earlier planning decisions for the house, bring those out, but we can find most of what we need ourselves.

Who will I be working with?

Martin and our small team. You deal with the same people from the first visit to the final drawing, and you always know who to call.

How long does the design take?

It depends on the size of the project and how many options you want to explore. We give you a realistic timetable at the start, once we have seen the property and understood the brief.

Planning permission

Do I always need planning permission for an extension?

Not always. Many extensions can be built under permitted development rights. As a guide, a single storey rear extension can go out 3 metres on a terraced or semi-detached house and 4 metres on a detached house, or up to 6 and 8 metres through the council's prior approval process. These rights are restricted in conservation areas, on listed buildings and where earlier extensions have used them up. We check your position at the start.

What is a lawful development certificate?

A certificate from the council confirming that work is permitted development and does not need planning permission. It is not compulsory, but it gives you certainty before you build and is useful when you come to sell. We prepare and submit these regularly.

How long does planning permission take?

Councils aim to decide householder applications within eight weeks of validating them, and major applications within 13 weeks. Some councils take longer in busy periods. We follow up with the planning officer for you and keep you updated.

Will my neighbours be told?

Yes. The council writes to neighbours or puts up a site notice, and they normally have 21 days to comment. It often helps to tell them about your plans first, and we can advise on how a design affects them.

What if the council asks for changes?

That is part of the job. We discuss any concerns with the planning officer and, where it helps, revise the drawings during the application rather than letting it be refused.

What happens if permission is refused?

You can appeal to the Planning Inspectorate (for householder applications, within 12 weeks of the decision), revise the design to address the reasons for refusal and apply again, or ask the council for pre-application advice. We will recommend the best route based on the reasons given.

What is a pre-application enquiry?

A request to the council for its view on a proposal before a full application is made. It is usually paid. It can be worthwhile for larger or sensitive schemes, and we can tell you whether it is worth it for yours.

My house is in a conservation area. Does that change things?

Yes. Permitted development rights are more limited in conservation areas, and some streets have extra controls (called Article 4 directions). Listed buildings also need listed building consent. Design matters even more, and we know the local guidance well.

How long does planning permission last?

Normally three years from the date of the decision. Work has to start within that time.

Extensions and lofts

Can I extend a semi-detached house?

Yes. Single storey rear extensions are often possible under permitted development. Side and two storey extensions usually need planning permission. We will go through the options for your property when we meet.

Do I need planning permission for a loft conversion?

Many loft conversions are permitted development, provided the extra roof space is no more than 40 cubic metres on a terraced house or 50 cubic metres on a detached or semi-detached house. Dormers facing the road need permission, as do most roof changes in conservation areas.

Do I need building regulations approval?

Almost always. Extensions, loft conversions and garage conversions need building regulations approval even when they do not need planning permission. We prepare the building control drawings and specification after planning approval.

Do I need a party wall agreement?

If the work involves a shared wall, or digging foundations close to a neighbour's, the Party Wall Act means you must give your neighbours notice before you start: two months for work on a shared wall and one month for excavations. We point out where it applies and can recommend party wall surveyors.

Do you work with structural engineers?

Yes. We coordinate the structural design with engineers we work with regularly, so the drawings your builder receives are complete.

Working with us

How much will it cost?

Every project is different, so we talk fees through with you in person once we understand what you want to do, and then confirm a fixed quote. Council application fees are paid separately. Call 07843 561129 for a chat.

Do you deal with the council for me?

Yes. We submit the application as your agent and deal with the planning officer from start to finish, so you do not have to.

Do you prepare building control drawings?

Yes. After planning approval we prepare the detailed drawings needed for building regulations, including construction details, specification and drainage, and coordinate structural calculations where needed.

Can you recommend a builder?

Yes. We have worked with reliable builders across North London and Hertfordshire for many years and are happy to suggest some when you reach the build stage.

Can I see work you have done near me?

Yes. Our project map shows hundreds of approved projects, each linked to the council's record, and you can search them by street, town or type of work.

Which areas do you cover?

Mainly North London and Hertfordshire, and further afield by arrangement. See areas we cover.
